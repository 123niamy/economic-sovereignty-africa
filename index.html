What is Pan-Africanism?

Pan-Africanism is a political, cultural and social movement that promotes the solidarity of all people of African descent. It emerged in response to slavery, colonialism and racial oppression, and affirms that Africans must define and govern their own future.

From the diaspora of the Americas and the Caribbean to the independence struggles on the continent, it has inspired generations to demand freedom, economic self-reliance, cultural pride and continental integration.

Core Principles

Unity

Africans stronger together: political, economic and cultural integration across borders.

Sovereignty

Self-determination, freedom from colonial and neo-colonial domination.

Economic Independence

Industrialisation, intra-African trade and control of Africa's own resources.

Cultural Pride

Celebrating African languages, heritage, knowledge and identity.

Diaspora Solidarity

Connecting Africa with people of African descent worldwide.

Justice & Equality

Dignity, human rights and opportunity for every African.

A Brief History

  1. 1900 First Pan-African Conference, London, organised by Henry Sylvester Williams.
  2. 1914–1920s Marcus Garvey's UNIA spreads “Africa for the Africans” worldwide.
  3. 1919–1945 Pan-African Congresses led by W.E.B. Du Bois; the 1945 Manchester Congress energises independence movements.
  4. 1957 Ghana, under Kwame Nkrumah, becomes independent, inspiring the continent.
  5. 1963 The Organisation of African Unity (OAU) is founded in Addis Ababa.
  6. 2002 The African Union replaces the OAU.
  7. 2013 Agenda 2063 adopted as Africa's 50-year development blueprint.
  8. 2018 African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) agreement signed.

Voices of the Movement

Kwame Nkrumah

Ghana's first president and champion of a United States of Africa.

Marcus Garvey

Jamaican leader who mobilised millions around Black pride and self-reliance.

W.E.B. Du Bois

Scholar and activist, “father of Pan-Africanism”.

Thomas Sankara

Burkina Faso's revolutionary president, advocate of self-sufficiency and dignity.

Patrice Lumumba

Congo's first prime minister, symbol of anti-colonial resistance.

Julius Nyerere

Tanzania's founding president, architect of African solidarity and Ujamaa.

Pan-Africanism Today

The movement now focuses on the African Union's Agenda 2063, the AfCFTA, free movement of people, digital and industrial development, youth leadership, and strengthening ties with the diaspora.

New generations are using technology, art, music and entrepreneurship to build the Africa they want to see: connected, innovative and self-determined.
